THE ROADSIDE FIELD GUIDE

Car cranks but won’t start

The engine turns at its usual speed but never catches. Fuel, ignition, key authorization, timing, and engine signals can produce overlapping symptoms.

Start these guided checks →

Stay safe first

Park safely, apply the parking brake, and use your owner’s manual. Stop if there is smoke, fuel smell, a leak, or a damaged battery. Keep clear of moving parts. No starting fluid, starter tapping, wiring bypasses, or work underneath the vehicle. EV and hybrid high-voltage systems are outside this guide.

1. Check fuel history

A low or inaccurate fuel gauge can hide an empty tank. If this began after refueling and wrong fuel is possible, do not restart. Diesel vehicles that run dry may need a model-specific priming procedure.

2. Notice security warnings

A key or padlock warning can help narrow the checks. Compare it with the manual, because a flashing security light can be normal when parked.

3. Listen to what already happened

Unusually fast, uneven, or metallic cranking deserves an inspection. Do not crank again to reproduce it. A stationary RPM needle alone does not establish a failed crankshaft sensor.

Does a scanner prove which part failed?

No. Stored codes add clues, but some no-start faults produce no code. Fuel, spark, compression, and engine signals may need professional testing.

Know when to stop

If you are unsure, save your observations and contact roadside assistance or a qualified technician. This educational guide does not confirm a failed part. No qualified automotive technician review has been verified.

Updated November 7, 2026. Reference: your model-year owner’s manual and AAA auto repair resources.
